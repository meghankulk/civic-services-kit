Kaizen Service Ontology · case study

From one goal to every screen.

Before building a product, model it. This ontology starts from what Kaizen says it does, the people who use and run its services, and what they need. It traces each need to a requirement, then to the platform block that builds it, the kit pattern that shows it, the design check that guards it and the public program where it's already live.

Users

Who it serves

Seven kinds of users across three surfaces. Residents are the primary user. Everyone else keeps the service running.

Platform

Blocks every program reuses

Kaizen describes Taproot as a library of building blocks: payments, registrations, identity, triage and approvals, reporting. Mapping requirements to blocks shows which ones carry the most weight, and which a new program gets for free.

Open questions

What I'd ask the team

Each one is a place the public record stops. These are where my model could be wrong.

Method

How it was built

1. Read what's public. Kaizen's site and press, the Maryland and Pima apps, the Colorado partner listing, and DoD releases on the C-UAS Marketplace.

2. Use the live apps. Every "stated" requirement on the civic side was seen in a working Kaizen app on Nov 20, 2026.

3. Model the rest. Hollow dots mark my design reading. Each one is a hypothesis to check with the team.

4. Tie it to the kit. Each requirement points to a kit pattern. Three patterns aren't built yet, and those are the next ones to design.

      Design study · November 2026
      
Components for public services people actually finish.

      
A component library built from what Kaizen ships in public. The resident platform (MUI, DM Sans, a single deep-teal action color, 16px cards on a #F7F7F7 band) sits next to the company site's dark, quiet type. Every part below is live: click it, filter it, book it.

Maps
The map and the list stay in sync. Pick a row and the map pans to it, or pick a pin and its row highlights. Nearby pins merge into a count until you zoom in. The basemap is drawn locally and the county is fictional.
